A day in the game lasts 20 minutes. The clock times below follow the F3 clock, where sunrise is 06:00 and sunset 18:00. See [[page:time-and-weather]].

## A villager's day
- **Morning.** A villager that slept indoors walks out through the door, keeps going for about 5 blocks straight away from it, and the door shuts behind it. This includes a child, and a house at the edge of the village whose door faces away from the centre: only after those 5 blocks does a villager that far out turn back toward the village.
- **Working hours, about 07:00 to 16:50.** Villagers with a workstation walk to it now and then and stand beside it for 8 to 20 seconds, which is when many crafters actually craft. In between, each does the job described on its profession page: the [[villager:farmer]] tends fields, the [[villager:builder]] builds, the [[villager:miner]] digs. A villager with nothing to do wanders within about 40 blocks of its bed, or the village centre, opening and closing doors as it goes. One that finds itself standing in a closed doorway opens that door too before it sets off.
- **Meals** at about 07:00, 11:15 and 16:05.
- **Shopping and farming** carry on until sunset. Builders stop a little earlier, at about 17:15.
- **Evening, from 18:00 to the next dawn.** Everyone heads for bed and sleeps.
- **Travellers.** [[villager:merchant|Merchants]] and [[villager:explorer|explorers]] who are caught far from home at dusk pitch a tent and sleep in it, and strike camp in the morning.

The trade screen has a line saying what the villager is doing right now, such as "Harvesting Wheat", "Buying food" or "Resting".

## Beds and sleep
Most villagers have a bed in the house they live in. A villager without a usable bed (a broken bed, a builder or explorer with no home yet, a child) claims the nearest free bed in the village at night. **No villager ever takes a bed another villager holds.** A bedless villager that cannot find a free bed goes inside the nearest building with room, or stands by the bell.

## Food and hunger
A villager eats the equivalent of one bread a day, a child two, in three meals. Every food you can eat counts, and the villager eats the cheapest per bread first, rotten flesh and raw food before bread. You can see crumbs and hear it eat when you are near.

- A villager that holds less than a day's food and has an emerald walks to the villager with the most spare food, farmers first, and buys up to three days' worth. A seller always keeps about 7 bread's worth for itself. See [[page:village-economy]].
- Someone who goes more than a day without finding food is **starving**. A starving villager only trades food ("Too hungry to trade").
- A hungry [[villager:unemployed|unemployed villager]] buys food from you: it pays an emerald for food worth about 88 percent of an emerald, until it holds three days of food.
- A villager who can cook buys eggs and cooks them in a furnace; one who cannot buys ready food. Raw eggs are not food.
- A villager buys a **treat**, a cake slice or a pumpkin pie from the [[villager:baker]], at most every 3 days and only with 2 emeralds to spare. It eats one a day at most.
- Food is never created for them: starting food is a few bread, apples, carrots or baked potatoes, farmers grow their own.

## Children and breeding
A village can only grow while it has more beds than villagers. Two adult villagers of the same village who stand within 5 blocks of each other roll a 50 percent chance, once per meeting, to have a child, provided both:

- are awake, not starving, and hold at least 7 bread's worth of food;
- have not bred in the last day.

Any profession can breed. A successful pair stops, face each other and hearts rise for a couple of seconds, then a child appears holding 10 bread, 5 from each parent. The child is about half size, runs faster and cannot trade. It eats 2 bread a day, so after about 5 days its bread is gone and it grows up. It then becomes unemployed and heads for a free workstation, preferring the professions of its parents. A village stops growing at 40 villagers, or at one and a half times its starting population if that is more.

At dusk a child takes the nearest free bed, or stands by the bell.

## Jobsites and job memory
An unemployed villager looks every few seconds for the nearest free workstation within 48 blocks of the village centre and walks to it. It takes the job once it stands beside the block, and whoever arrives first gets it. A job gives its profession's starting wares, apart from emeralds, the first time only. Villagers that start a farm, forestry, mining or shepherd job later get the money for a tool instead. [[villager:nitwit|Nitwits]] never take a job. A villager that has earned experience only takes its own profession's workstation.

A village can hold only one [[villager:stable_hand]], at most one [[villager:merchant]] for every 20 villagers and one [[villager:baker]] for every 15.

**If a workstation is broken**, its villager becomes unemployed but remembers the profession, keeping its level, experience and inventory. If you place a workstation of that profession within 30 days, the nearest villager who remembers it takes it at once, and remembering villagers get first pick of such blocks. After 30 days it forgets: level and experience reset, and it is a plain unemployed villager.

## Levels and experience
See [[page:villagers]]: five levels, experience earned from trades with you and with other villagers.

## Chests and storage
Villagers with a full inventory put things in a chest. An unclaimed chest belongs to whoever first puts an item in it (or takes one out). A villager only uses chests that are unclaimed or its own, and only in the house its bed is in. It stores its surplus, least needed first, never emeralds or tools, and takes things back when it runs low. A villager far from home when it fills up walks back to its chest in stages, so it does not give up on a chest that is only far away. If the house has no chest it can use it orders one: a [[villager:furniture_maker]] makes it from 8 planks and puts it in the house for 1 emerald. A chest that stays empty for a day, or whose villager owner dies, is unclaimed again.

In survival a chest owned by someone else is look-only for you.

## Happiness
The debug screen shows a happiness score for each village, out of 100. It loses 10 for each villager killed this week, 5 for each starving villager now, and 3 for each unemployed or penniless adult. It gains 1 for each trade this week, 5 for each birth or child grown up this week and 1 for each villager that ate a treat in the last 3 days. It only reports, and changes nothing.

## Death
A villager killed stays dead. Its slot in the village is not refilled, only births replace it, and each village needs a spare bed for that. Its chests are unclaimed. Hurting a villager angers the village's iron golems.
