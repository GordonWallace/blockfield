title: Village economy
category: Villagers

A village runs on one market. Every trade a [[page:villagers|villager]] makes with another villager is an offer on its trade screen, and you can take the same offer yourself. There are no secret deals between villagers: when a [[villager:builder]] buys planks from a [[villager:forester]], it pays the forester's price, uses up the forester's stock and gives the forester experience, exactly as you would.

## Offers
Each villager's trade screen shows three kinds of offer:

- **Job offers.** The wares of its profession, with more of them at higher levels (see [[page:villagers]]). A villager sells a ware only while it holds it, so these offers run out.
- **Spare goods.** Anything a villager holds beyond what it needs that its job offers do not already sell is put on sale too, in lots of about one emerald. The price is the cheapest base price any villager charges for that item, or the item's worth plus 5 percent if no one sells it. This is how a leftover empty bottle or a pile of raw copper finds a buyer.
- **Needs.** When a villager lacks something right now, it posts an offer to buy it: food when it is down to less than a day's worth, a tool of its trade it has none of, wheat for a shepherd short of feed, or building materials a builder is short of. Needs pay 88 percent of an item's worth. Other villagers fill needs by buying through each other's sell offers, and you can fill them directly.
- **One side per item.** A villager is either selling an item or looking for it, never both at once. Where its offers would buy and sell the same thing (an armorer's lanterns, a farmer's carrots, a cleric's bones once it has a pile of them), it sells while it can spare a full lot and buys otherwise. The other side's offers are hidden from the trade screen until its stock changes, and their prices wait where they were.

## Reserves
Each villager keeps what its next moves require, and never sells it, to villagers or to you. The trade screen says "Keeping for own use". A villager keeps:

- a week's worth of food (about 7 bread) and the tools of its job;
- seed for a [[villager:farmer]], feed for a [[villager:shepherd]], the materials left on the structure a [[villager:builder]] is building and on the one it has chosen next;
- the inputs of its next two crafts, for crafters such as the [[villager:toolsmith]], [[villager:furniture_maker]] and [[villager:cartographer]].

What it keeps is worked out from what it holds at that moment, so goods it has just bought count at once: they are never offered as spare goods the instant the deal is done.

## Stock and purses
A villager's stock of each ware is capped at a few batches of the offer size (six at Novice, down to two at Master, at most two stacks and about six emeralds' worth). Emeralds trickle in at 2 a day up to 12 for most villagers. Builders get 4 a day up to 80, because they pay for whole houses.

## Prices follow demand
Every emerald offer drifts with shortage and surplus, for you and for villagers alike. A price moves in steps of about 2.3 percent, up to 30 steps either way, which is a doubling or a halving.

- **A buy offer** (the villager pays emeralds for goods) that goes unfilled for a day, while the villager could pay and has room, offers one step more.
- **A sell offer** (the villager sells a ware) whose ware sits at its stock cap for a day without a sale drops one step.
- **Each trade** that fills an offer moves its price three steps back toward the base price.

A month of constant shortage or glut takes a price to its limit. Prices only change once a day, never in the middle of a trade. Two floors keep the market sane: a villager only ever has one side of an item open (see above), and it never buys above the cheapest base price any villager charges for it. Offers with no emerald on one side (barter) and the explorer's maps keep fixed prices.

Prices matter for [[page:caravans]] too: a [[villager:merchant]] carries goods from a village where a price has fallen to one where it has risen.

## Nothing from nothing
The aim is that goods have a source. A villager sells a ware only if it made, grew, dug or bought it:

- Food is never conjured. Farmers harvest crops and bake them, and villagers buy food from each other. A butcher or fisherman cooks the raw meat or cod it already holds.
- Metal, stone, wood, wool, leather, glass, honey and tools are dug, felled, sheared, smelted or crafted by the matching profession from materials it bought.
- Builders place only blocks taken from their own inventory, and only the wood their structure is built from. See [[villager:builder]].

A few wares are still topped up by a daily restock, by about a quarter of their cap a day:

- the [[villager:librarian]]: paper and books;
- the [[villager:cartographer]]: paper;
- the [[villager:mason]]: bricks, brick blocks, stone, sandstone bricks and six kinds of terracotta.

Everything else a villager sells comes from stock it was born with, from what it makes, or from what you or another villager sold it. Starting stock is also restricted: a profession that makes its wares never starts with them, so a new [[villager:toolsmith]] has no tools and a new [[villager:cowherd]] no milk.

## Villagers feeding villagers
Hungry villagers shop for food from the villager with the most surplus, farmers first, and a seller always keeps its own 7 bread's worth. A villager with a furnace nearby buys eggs and cooks them; one without buys ready food. See [[page:villager-life]].
